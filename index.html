The product

Everything between your chip
and your customer.

The serving stack your customers expect, built on your chip’s low-level toolchain.

01

An OpenAI-compatible API.

Chat and completions endpoints with SSE streaming, stop strings, seeds and sampling controls, tested against OpenAI’s own Python client.

02

Continuous batching.

Requests join and leave the batch every step, with chunked prefill and preemption. Each request’s tokens are bit-identical to running it alone.

03

Paged KV cache.

Attention memory is allocated in fixed-size blocks, so long prompts and many users share memory without fragmenting it.

04

Quantization, measured.

Per-channel INT8 and FP8 (E4M3) weights, and INT4 through AWQ and GPTQ checkpoints. We publish the perplexity of every format.

05

Conformance reports.

Every operation and every model is checked against PyTorch and Hugging Face references, and you get a report you can share with customers.

And vLLM?

vLLM needs a ready chip.
We get your chip ready.

We’re fans. vLLM is an excellent open-source engine, and on NVIDIA it’s faster than FuseZap today. But in practice, a chip joins the vLLM world only after its maker has built the foundation underneath: framework support for the operations models use, and hand-tuned kernels for the parts that matter. That foundation is the months of work FuseZap takes on.

Going it alone, your team builds

  • PyTorch or JAX support for your chip, one operation at a time
  • Hand-tuned kernels for attention, matrix multiply and every new model feature
  • A vLLM plugin that keeps pace as vLLM changes
  • Your own way to prove each release still works

With FuseZap, your team brings

  • The kernel compiler and driver you already have
  • Your tuned kernels, if you’d like us to use them
  • We generate the rest from about 28 basic operations
  • Validate proves every model after every release

It doesn’t have to be either-or. You can keep vLLM for serving, and we’re building Validate to check any engine, vLLM included. One honest caveat: our claim that the second model costs far less than the first is exactly what our Inferentia2 work is measuring now. More on this

Your SDK changes. A driver ships. A new model lands. Each time, someone has to check that every model still gives the right answers at the right speed. Usually it’s one of your best engineers, by hand. Validate does it every time, and shows its work.

FAQ

What chip software teams
ask us first.

vLLM says it runs any model on any hardware. Why FuseZap?

vLLM is excellent, and on NVIDIA it’s faster than us today. But in practice a chip only joins vLLM after its maker builds PyTorch or JAX support and hand-tuned kernels. That’s the months of work FuseZap takes on: you bring a kernel compiler and a driver, we generate the rest, and Validate proves every release. More above.

Do you replace our compiler or runtime?

Partly, so here’s exactly which part. We use your low-level toolchain and driver to compile and launch kernels, and we call any tuned kernels you already have. We replace the graph-level layers above them: fusion, scheduling, kernel generation and serving. Everything that touches your silicon directly stays yours.

How long does it take to get a model running?

It depends on how mature your toolchain is. Our goal is weeks, not quarters, for a first model. After a first call, we’ll give you an honest estimate.

Who owns the code for our chip?

You do. The code written for your chip is yours, and we can hand it over in full, with its tests, so your team can run it without us.

What does it cost?

The assessment is free. Work together starts with a 90-day pilot, paid in four stages tied to results we agree up front, so you only pay for the stages you start and can stop at any gate. If you carry on, the pilot fee counts toward your first year: a yearly subscription to keep every model qualified, however many chips you ship. Our first design partners get a lower pilot price in return for being a reference.
