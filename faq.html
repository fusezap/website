Getting started

How long does it take to get a model running?

It depends on how mature your toolchain is. Our goal is weeks, not quarters, for a first model. After a first call, we’ll give you an honest estimate.

Can we start before we have silicon?

Yes. If your functional simulator exposes the same compile-and-launch path as your driver, we can build the backend and prove correctness on it, op by op and model by model. Performance tuning waits for real hardware, so you’re ready to tune the day boards arrive.

What if our toolchain can’t handle something?

Then we find out in the first weeks, not at launch. A project starts with a conformance run that shows which operations, data types and shapes your toolchain handles. We generate kernels in your kernel language where we can, and flag clearly what needs your engineers.

How fast do you support a new model?

It depends on what’s new. A new release in a model family we already support, like a new Llama or Qwen version, is usually new weights on a familiar design, so we aim to have it running and qualified within days. A genuinely new design, such as a new kind of attention or a mixture-of-experts model, takes longer, because the compiler may need new building blocks. We’ll publish real turnaround times as we collect them, and agreed response times are written into your contract.

How it works

Why not just write a vLLM plugin?

You can, and some teams do. A plugin gives you vLLM’s scheduler and API, but your engineers still write and tune kernels for each model’s operations, and keep up as vLLM changes every few weeks. FuseZap generates and tunes kernels for your chip from about 28 primitive operations, so a new model should need little or no new kernel work. We’re measuring exactly that on a real accelerator now, and we’ll publish the number.

Do you replace our compiler or runtime?

Partly, so here’s exactly which part. We use your low-level toolchain and driver to compile and launch kernels, and we call any tuned kernels you already have. We replace the graph-level layers above them: fusion, scheduling, kernel generation and serving. Everything that touches your silicon directly stays yours.

How do you prove it’s right?

We compare against PyTorch and Hugging Face at every level: single operations, whole models token for token, and perplexity for every number format. You get the report, and the tests run in your CI too.

What happens when we ship a new SDK or driver?

Validate reruns every supported model against the reference, in every number format, and compares accuracy, speed and memory with the last release. You get a pass, or the exact failure and which versions it affects, before your customers find it.

Is it ready for production?

Not for a large fleet yet, and we’d rather say so. Today the focus is getting models running correctly on new chips. Metrics, tracing and access control come from standard tools you likely already run, such as Prometheus, OpenTelemetry and an API gateway. The rest, like rollouts and failure recovery, we build with our first design partners. The engineering notes list what exists today.

Ownership, trust and cost

Who owns the code for our chip?

You do. The code written for your chip is yours, and we can hand it over in full, with its tests, so your team can run it without us.

How do you protect our IP?

We work under NDA, on your hardware or in your environment if you prefer. Nothing about your chip goes into work we do for anyone else.

What if FuseZap goes away?

You won’t be stranded. The code written for your chip is yours. The FuseZap compiler core is open source under Apache 2.0, and pilot customers get it from the day they sign, ahead of our public release. The serving engine and Validate are covered by source-code escrow: if FuseZap ever goes out of business, an independent escrow agent releases their source to you, so you can keep supporting your own chips. The exact terms are in your contract.

Who do we call when something goes wrong?

Promod Gangadharan, our CEO, is accountable for your delivery and is your named contact for anything that needs escalating. Support runs 8am to 5pm Pacific, Monday to Friday.

Does FuseZap use AI, or send our data to an AI service?

The product doesn’t. FuseZap Core, the serving engine and Validate run entirely on your systems and never call an AI service. Our engineers do use AI coding tools in their work. When that work touches your confidential material, your contract sets the rules: only providers that don’t train on your data, named up front, and you can mark anything off-limits or have the tools run in your own environment.

Are there royalties? Does FuseZap send data back to you?

No per-device royalties. You pay to bring your chip up and a subscription to keep models qualified, however many chips you ship. And FuseZap doesn’t send usage data, model data or anything else back to us; it runs entirely in your environment. Downloading a public model talks to Hugging Face, not to us, and you can point FuseZap at local copies instead.

What does it cost?

The assessment is free. Work together starts with a 90-day pilot, paid in four stages tied to results we agree up front, so you only pay for the stages you start and can stop at any gate. If you carry on, the pilot fee counts toward your first year: a yearly subscription to keep every model qualified, however many chips you ship. Our first design partners get a lower pilot price in return for being a reference.
